The still

Four hundred and eighty litres of copper, run once a week.

The still was built in Hamburg in 1927 and has stood in this building since 1954. It holds 480 litres, it is heated by steam rather than by flame, and it has been rebricked twice without any change to the shape of the neck.

One run a week, 620 bottles. The heads and the tails are cut on tasting and confirmed against the hydrometer, and only the middle cut is bottled. Everything outside it is redistilled with the following batch rather than thrown away.

  • ISteep overnight, cold
  • IISlow rise, four hours to first drip
  • IIICut on tasting, twice
  • IVRest three weeks in steel

Serves

Four ways, and one of them is nothing.

Fifty millilitres in a small tulip glass at room temperature. This is how we taste every run and it is the only way to find out whether you actually like gin or whether you like tonic.

  • 50 ml Iron Hare
  • Nothing else

Stirred, never anything else, and colder than you think is reasonable. The pine tip in the botanical bill does the work that a garnish usually has to.

  • 60 ml Iron Hare
  • 10 ml dry vermouth
  • Stir 30 seconds over block ice
  • Lemon peel, expressed and discarded

A tall glass, a lot of ice, and half as much tonic as you have been taught to pour. The ratio is one to two, not one to four.

  • 50 ml Iron Hare
  • 100 ml Indian tonic
  • Rowan berry, if it is autumn

Our own, named after the boat the still arrived on. Slightly sweet, slightly bitter, and it improves for about an hour if you leave it alone.
