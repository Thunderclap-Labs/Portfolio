01

The shell is a heatsink that happens to be a shell.

Anodised 7075 with a 1.8 mm wall and twenty eight fins cut straight into the barrel. There is no separate heatsink because a separate heatsink is a thermal joint, and a thermal joint is a place where your continuous rating quietly disappears.

  • Material7075‑T6, hard anodised
  • Wall1.8 mm
  • Fins28, machined not bonded
  • SealingIP65 with the M12 cap fitted

02

Copper, and an unfashionable amount of it.

Twelve slots, 0.63 mm wire, 84 turns a tooth, wound on a machine we own rather than one we rent. Slot fill is 62 per cent, which is the number that decides whether the continuous rating on this page is honest.

  • Slots12, distributed
  • Turns84 per tooth
  • Slot fill62 %
  • ClassH, 180 °C

03

The encoder is the product. The motor is the easy part.

A 21‑bit absolute encoder on the rotor and a 17‑bit on the output, so the drive knows the difference between a joint that moved and a joint that flexed. Commutation, current loop and thermal model all run on this board at 40 kHz.

  • Rotor side21 bit absolute
  • Output side17 bit absolute
  • Loop rate40 kHz current, 8 kHz position
  • BusEtherCAT or CAN FD

04

Three planets, one sun, and no cycloid mysticism.

A planetary set with ground helical teeth, because it is serviceable, it is quiet, and the efficiency curve is something we can publish rather than imply. Choose one, two or three stages when you configure the unit.

  • TypePlanetary, ground helical
  • Ratios1:9, 1:36, 1:121
  • Efficiency94 % at rated load
  • Backlash3 arcmin, measured per unit
