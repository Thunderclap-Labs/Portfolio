Giria

Three nights of songs in a pine wood an hour from Kaunas. No amplification after midnight, no headliner, and a fire that somebody has to keep going until morning.

Keep scrolling, the wood gets darker

The wood

You walk in from the road and the noise stops.

The site is nine hundred metres of sand track from the nearest tarmac, which is the single most important thing about it. By the time you reach the clearing you have already slowed down, and the wood has done most of the work that a festival programme usually has to do.

There are two places to hear something. The clearing has a low wooden stage and a string of bulbs. The fire, forty metres into the trees, has no stage at all, and that is where most of it happens.

Songbook

Learn four of them before you come.

These four get sung every year and nobody announces them. Open one and the lanterns come up. Words are ours, tunes are old and belong to nobody.

Nine hundred metres of loose grey sand,
a bag on my back and a cup in my hand.
The road went quiet a mile ago,
and whatever I brought, I am putting down.

Two chords. Whoever is at the front sets the pace, and the pace is walking pace.

Somebody has to keep it going,
somebody has to stay awake.
Put another arm of pine on,
the morning is not far away.

Call and response. The fire keeper sings the first line and everybody gives it back.

No one is bigger than the wood tonight,
no one is louder than the rain.
We put our names in the same tin,
and we pull them out again.

The running order really is drawn from a tin. This is the song about the tin.

Take the ash out to the birches,
fold the canvas while it is dry.
Whatever we found in here this weekend,
carry it out, do not let it lie.

Slow. Everybody stands. It is the only moment of the weekend that is organised.

Getting there and staying

Everything you need to know is short.

The track

Bus 27 from Kaunas to Girionys, then the sand track east. It is nine hundred metres and it is not lit. Bring a small torch and use it pointing down.

Sleeping

Camping is in the ticket. Pitch under the pines on the south side, two metres apart, and take everything out with you on Sunday.

The fire

One fire, always lit, always someone minding it. If you sit down at it you are on the rota. That is the only rule we enforce.

Quiet

Amplified sound stops at midnight and does not come back until four in the afternoon. Unamplified singing has never stopped and never will.
